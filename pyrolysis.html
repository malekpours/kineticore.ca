Why pyrolysis, why now

A Circular Process for a Linear Problem

Every year, sawmills, municipalities and land-clearing operations dispose of enormous volumes of wood waste — landfilled, burned open-air, or left to decompose and release carbon. Pyrolysis changes that equation: heating biomass in an oxygen-free environment locks carbon into a stable, useful solid instead of releasing it to the atmosphere.

Kineticore's advantage is measurement. A biochar product is only as valuable as its documented process — and process documentation is precisely what we have built our company on.

  • Diverts wood waste from landfill and open burning
  • Locks carbon into stable biochar for decades to centuries
  • Recovers syngas and heat to power the process itself
  • Produces measurable, auditable carbon removal
The process

Five Stages, Fully Instrumented

From raw residue to finished carbon product — each stage is monitored by Kineticore DAQ hardware.

Stage by stage

What Happens Inside the Reactor

1

Feedstock Prep

Wood waste is received, screened for contaminants, dried and sized to consistent specifications.

2

Pyrolysis

Biomass is heated to 350–700 °C in an oxygen-free reactor. Solids, condensables and gas separate.

3

Separation & Cooling

Vapors are condensed into bio-oil; non-condensable syngas is captured; char is cooled under controlled conditions.

4

Quality Control

Each batch is sampled and characterized — moisture, carbon content, pH, particle size — before release.

5

Dispatch

Finished biochar is packaged and shipped with its batch record, ready for soil, filtration or industrial use.

Products

One Reactor, Four Outputs

Biochar

Our primary product: stable carbon for soil amendment, filtration, construction materials and carbon removal credits. Sold with per-batch lab documentation.

Syngas

Non-condensable gases (CO, H₂, CH₄) are captured and recycled to fuel the reactor — minimizing external energy input.

Bio-Oil

Condensed wood vinegar and tar fractions with potential as bio-based feedstock, soil conditioner or preservative.

Heat

Reactor surplus heat is recovered for feedstock drying and facility heating — closing the energy loop.
